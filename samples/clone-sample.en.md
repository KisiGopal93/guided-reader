## Part 1 - The question

How would you teach a computer to spot a cat? Your answer might surprise you. It can also change how you see AI.

## Part 2 - The rulebook

People often picture AI as a smart robot brain. Or they think it is a program full of clever rules. Old software really was a rulebook. A programmer had to write every rule, an exact instruction, by hand.

Say you want software to spot a cat. You write a rule that it must have four legs. A cow has four legs too. So you add pointy ears and a tail. Now a dog fits your rules. A fox fits them as well, and nobody wants a fox in their living room. Then a strange photo shows a cat curled into a ball, and your rules break.

## Part 3 - The real world

The real world is always messy like that. Think of a busy Kathmandu road. A cow is standing right in the middle of the lane. A scooter squeezes past it on the left. You could write thousands of rules for traffic. Real life will still surprise you every day.

## Part 4 - How a child learns

Now watch how a small child learns the same thing. Nobody hands a toddler a rulebook about ears and tails. You just point at examples. You point to a cat sitting on a wall. Later, you show her a sleeping kitten. Another day, she sees a cartoon cat in a book.

Soon she knows what a cat looks like. She cannot tell you a single rule. She simply found the pattern. A pattern is what many examples have in common.
